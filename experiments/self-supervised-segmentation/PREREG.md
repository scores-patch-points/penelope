# PREREG (EXPLORATORY): does a self-supervised surprise criterion recover which clitic splits help, and does it adapt to the source without a hand rule?
Written 2026-11-20 BEFORE any run. Nothing here is measured. The owner's principle that motivates it: programs should not be hand-written for every little thing; they should develop on their own, weighted by how much less surprising they make the text (predictive processing). This is the smallest test of that principle that can fail.
## Data (read from the local git history of Zenodotus at commit d79ee5a; nothing is downloaded and the sparse checkout is not changed)
INFORMAL (typos, slang, abbreviations; real human typing): `19-organic-community/nus-sms/en/*`, `19-organic-community/cosem/*`, `19-organic-community/ubuntu-irc/{ubuntu,kubuntu,xubuntu,ubuntu-server}/*`, in sorted path order; YAML frontmatter removed. EDITED (control): `01-literature-books/gutenberg/*.txt` whose frontmatter or name marks English, first six in sorted order, excluding any file named Greek/Latin/translation. Each side is cut to the SAME number of tokens (the smaller side's count, first-come in sorted order) so frequency effects are comparable.
## The criterion (no hand-written rule per form)
Tokens are what khora's generic tokenizer gives (it keeps "Oscar's" and "don't" glued, as a plain tokenizer does). CANDIDATES are generated, not listed: every string b of 1 to 4 characters that is the final piece of at least two word types (a rule must apply to two instances to be a rule). Adopting b means splitting every type that ends in b (stem length at least 1) into stem + b. WEIGHT = description-length gain in bits: the unigram code length of the text before minus after, minus the cost of the types it removes and introduces (spelling cost = length x log2 of the alphabet size, derived from the text). This is the Morfessor two-part code; no number in it is chosen. A second signal is the DOWNSTREAM MODEL'S OWN CONFIDENCE: for sentences containing the form, the mean tagger margin (khora's tagWithMargins) with the split applied minus without, judged against a NULL made from the same text (a random word cut at the form's length in the same number of sentences, 100 draws, seeded), reported as the empirical rank (draws reaching the observed change + 1) / (draws + 1), with no cut-off.
## The reference (what the criterion is compared with, and why it is weak)
The codec session's PRELIMINARY smoke-run effects of splitting each clitic on parse accuracy of the other tokens (EWT dev, 10 null draws, DEV, not frozen): n't +5.8, 'm +3.3, 've +2.9, 's +2.7, 're +1.9, 'd 0.0, 'll -1.0. Seven numbers, one corpus, one parser, unfrozen: a rank correlation over seven points is almost uninformative (rho = 0.6 has p about 0.08) and is reported as such. The treebank gold stays what it was meant to be: a CHECK on the self-supervised criterion, never its source.
## Predictions and falsifiers
P1: on INFORMAL text the description-length gain ranks n't, 's, 've, 'm above 'd and 'll. F1: it does not.
P2: Spearman rho between the gain on informal text and the reference effect is at least 0.6. F2: rho below 0.4 (reported with n = 7 and its weakness).
P3: the apostrophe-less variants (the same seven forms with the apostrophe dropped: nt, s, m, ve, re, d, ll, generated by one operator, not listed by hand) gain MORE on INFORMAL text than on EDITED text for nt, ll, ve, re: the weight adapts to the source with no per-source rule. F3: they gain as much or more on edited text.
P4: for the genuine clitics (n't, 's, 've, 'm) the tagger-confidence change on informal text lies beyond the random-cut null (no null draw reaches it). F4: fewer than half of the seven forms have nullReaching = 0.
P5 (a finding either way): among the top 10 candidate suffixes by gain on EDITED text, most are ordinary morphology (s, ed, ing, ly, er ...), not clitics: description length alone proposes morphology. The tagger-confidence check must tell them apart: F5: splitting the plural -s ALSO raises tagger confidence beyond its null (then confidence does not discriminate clitics from plural morphology, and the downstream-difference criterion needs a stronger downstream signal such as the claim layer's own).
## What this does not claim
It does not claim a rule is right, or that the loop replaces the treebank evidence; it asks whether the criterion points the same way and moves with the source. One run, one tagger, two registers of English, a unigram code. A creole or another language needs its own text and codec: no such text is used here.
