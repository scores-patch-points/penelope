# PREREG-HELDOUT (EXPLORATORY): tier 1 of a two-tier signal: held-out predictive compression from our own tokens, calibrated on planted truth
Written 2026-11-20 BEFORE any run of heldout.mjs. The owner's principle: the prediction that judges a program should come from OUR OWN EOT-ized content, not from what a third-party model says; training wheels are accepted. Hence two tiers:
TIER 1 (this file; the proposer, training wheels): how much cheaper UNSEEN text becomes to predict under a model built only from our own ingested tokens, with a candidate cut applied. No tagger, no parser, nothing third-party. Cheap enough for ~1000 candidates.
TIER 2 (not built here; the judge, ours): surprise in our own EOT: the cost of the claims, beings and bonds the reader writes for unseen text, given the log so far (reusing a known being is cheap, a new one costly, a claim against a single-valued mark is a prediction error). Pre-registered when built. The tagger-confidence signal of earlier runs is retired from judging (kept only as a comparator) after the calibration showed the piece-identity artifact.
## The model (no free constants)
A generative model of the token stream of a text: P(w | v) by Witten-Bell interpolation of token bigram and unigram counts (BOS and EOS per sentence); a NEW token is spelled by a character bigram model trained on the training token TYPES with add-one smoothing over the alphabet plus an end symbol (the novelty mass is the number of types over tokens plus types: Witten-Bell). Cost = bits over a held-out fold; the denominator is the CHARACTERS of the held-out text (the same text under either tokenization), so bits per character are comparable across tokenizations. A candidate PROGRAM is a word-final cut (stem + suffix, stem at least one character) applied to the training tokens AND the held-out tokens. ΔBits of a program on a fold = bits(held-out under the program's model) minus bits(held-out under the baseline model), per sentence, so a paired bootstrap over sentences applies.
GOOD on a fold: of 1000 bootstrap resamples of the mean per-sentence ΔBits, none is >= 0 (the program lowers held-out cost beyond the sampling noise of the held-out sentences). REPLICATED: GOOD on at least two folds. Folds are leave-one-batch-out over the batches (each fold trains on the other batches and validates on one), so each validation is independent of the training it uses and of the other validations.
## Data (Zenodotus at commit d79ee5a, from git history; no download)
Calibration (conditions A, B, C as in PREREG-CALIBRATION): the first four batches of the informal training text (~13,340 tokens each), seed 20261120. A NOISE (every token's characters shuffled); B PLANTED (the five derived function words glued to their predecessor with probability 0.5, in all batches); C RARE (`zq` glued onto eight tokens of batch 1 only). Real text (condition D): the eight training batches of the informal text, 8 folds.
## Predictions and falsifiers
PA (calibrated): on A the share of evaluable candidates called GOOD on a fold is at most 1/31 (3.2%) and the share REPLICATED at most (1/31)^2 within sampling error. FA: GOOD above 2/31, or REPLICATED above 5 x (1/31)^2.
PB (it finds what is there): on B at least four of the five planted words are GOOD on at least two of the four folds. FB: fewer than three.
PC (content does not leak): `zq` is never GOOD on any fold. FC: it is GOOD on any fold.
PD1 (real, the clitics): n't and 's are GOOD on at least six of eight folds on the informal text. FD1: either on fewer than six.
PD2 (an honest expectation): the predictive signal ALSO calls morphology good: at least two of {s, ed, ing, ly} GOOD on at least six of eight folds, i.e. tier 1 cannot by itself tell clitics from morphology, and tier 2 (downstream, in our EOT) must. FD2: none is GOOD (then tier 1 is more selective than I expect, which would be reported as a finding).
## Not claimed
Predictive compression of tokens is not linguistic correctness; one language model family; planted text is not natural text; one source mix.
