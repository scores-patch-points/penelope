# PREREG-MIND (EXPLORATORY): does reading get cheaper and better as a MIND learns, and is the mind separate from the content?
Written 2026-11-20 BEFORE any run of mind.mjs. Nothing here is measured. The owner's instruction: wire the learned programs in, falsify that it helps, make reading cheaper and cheaper, and make the mind get smarter separately from the content that is ingested.
## Definitions
CONTENT = the text that was ingested (sentences, counts, vocabulary). MIND = a set of learned word-final cut programs `{ suffix, strength }` and nothing else: the programs a colony found worth keeping and what each was worth (Mind@1, khora adapters/text/mind-cuts.js, applied inside readingRows { mind }). The mind is kept as TRAILS: `trail-deposit` entries (SIG·Ground) in a log, read back by the pharos fold `trails`, ordered by khora's own kernel/stigmergy.js (evaporation, earned/untried/dead bands, scouts). A mind is rebuilt from the trails alone.
SURPRISE of reading a probe = minus the mean tagger margin (khora tagWithMargins, a FIXED model that ingestion cannot change) over the probe's tokens after the mind's cuts are applied. Lower is less surprising. Because the tagger is fixed, any change in surprise is attributable to the mind, never to the content having taught the model.
COST of learning = the number of program evaluations (each evaluation tags the sentences a program changes, with and without it) the colony spends on a batch before it stops. The stop is derived, not chosen: the colony stops a batch when the next M evaluations produce no new program that beats the null, M = the number of programs the mind already holds + 1.
## The ants (no hand-written program per phenomenon)
A candidate program is any word-final string of 1 to 4 characters that ends at least two word types in the batch. Each ant picks a candidate by the learned order (khora routeOrderFor over the trails for this stream; scouts with the kernel's own epsilon) and EVALUATES it: the mean tagger-margin change over a sample of the sentences it changes, minus the mean of a NULL for that cut length (random same-length cuts of random words in the same text, 30 draws, cached per cut length so it is paid once). ok = no null draw reaches the observed change; the deposit is { head: stream, route: "cut:<suffix>", ok, ms: evaluations, veto if the change is negative }. A route whose deposits are all vetoes goes dead (the kernel's own band).
## Data (Zenodotus at commit d79ee5a, read from git history; no download)
Informal streams: `nus-sms/en` (SMS), `cosem` (Singapore messages), `ubuntu-irc` (English channels). Edited: the six Gutenberg books of the first run. Training batches: cosem and nus-sms documents in sorted order, cut into 8 equal batches by tokens. PROBES (never ingested): ubuntu-irc English (a DIFFERENT source of the same register, for transfer), a held-out slice of cosem+nus (same source, unseen documents), and the edited books (a different register).
## Arms
MIND (ants, trails, null-guarded); NOMIND (glued, no cuts); RAND (10 minds of the same size as MIND's, programs drawn at random from the candidate set, no learning); RESET (MIND's final programs but with the trails thrown away and the batches re-ingested: shows the gain lives in the mind, not in having seen the text); CONTENT-ONLY (the batches ingested, nothing learned: identical to NOMIND by construction, reported to show that content alone moves nothing).
## Predictions and falsifiers
H1 (it helps): after the last batch, surprise on the unseen-source probe (ubuntu-irc) is lower under MIND than NOMIND, and no RAND mind reaches MIND's gain. F1: MIND's gain is not beyond the maximum of the 10 RAND minds, or is not positive.
H2 (cheaper and cheaper): the learning cost per batch (evaluations until the derived stop) falls across batches, and the surprise reduction per evaluation rises. F2: cost per batch in the last three batches is not lower than in the first three.
H3 (the mind is separate): (a) a reader given ONLY the mind rebuilt from the trails (no content, no batches) reproduces MIND's probe surprise EXACTLY. F3a: any difference. (b) the mind is small: its serialized size is under 1% of the bytes ingested. F3b: it is not. (c) the mind learned from the batches in a different order, and from different halves of the batches, converges on mostly the same programs: Jaccard of the top-5 programs is at least 0.5 between two independent runs (seeds differ). F3c: it is lower.
H4 (transfer and source-specificity): MIND's gain on the unseen-source informal probe is positive (transfer); on the edited probe it is smaller than on the informal probe, and not negative (it does no harm where it does not belong). F4a: no transfer. F4b: gain on the edited probe is as large as on informal, or negative (harm).
H5 (it learns what we know it should): the clitic cuts (n't, 's) are among the programs the mind holds after the last batch, found by the ants and not listed. F5: they are not, or only morphology (ed, ing, s) is. An honest note: a plural -s program that raises tagger confidence would mean confidence does not discriminate clitics from morphology (the earlier F5).
## Not claimed
Programs are word-final cuts only (SEG); joins (CON) and the helix dependency order belong to the codec session's multi-move colony (PREREG-ANT-COLONY-SURPRISE, janus), whose programs this mind can adopt when they exist. One run, one tagger, English only; no creole text is used here. A confidence gain is not a claim that a cut is linguistically right.
