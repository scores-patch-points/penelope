# self-supervised-segmentation (EXPLORATORY)
Does a self-supervised surprise criterion recover which clitic splits help, with no hand-written rule per form, on informal text (Zenodotus `19-organic-community`, read from git history, nothing downloaded)?
Files: `PREREG.md` (protocol, written before the run, sha256 first 16 `544fcb4594e5f70f`), `run.mjs`, `result.json`, `ERRATUM-1.md`. A second experiment (`PREREG-MIND.md`, `mind.mjs`) tests the learned mind and is not yet run to completion.
## Results (one run, one tagger, 360,474 tokens per side, seed 20261120)
- Description length alone (unigram code, Morfessor-style) does NOT favour clitic splits: n't -9503 bits, 'm -3874, 're -1001, 'll -823, 've -632; only 's +1980 and 'd +202; Spearman -0.54 against the preliminary treebank effects (n = 7). Falsifiers F1 and F2 hit.
- The same criterion proposes, with no rule or list, informal-specific cuts at the top of the informal ranking: a missing space after a full stop (".i"), character elongation ("eee", "hhh"), and on edited books the typographic "’s", "ness", "less", "est" and dash-joined words ("—and", "—but"): typography artifacts nobody listed.
- The downstream model's own confidence (mean tagger margin, split minus glued) against a random-cut null from the same text (100 draws) puts ALL SEVEN clitics beyond the null on informal text (0 of 100 null draws reach them): n't +4.52, 'm +3.65, 're +2.32, 'll +1.95, 've +1.92, 's +1.86, 'd +1.06. The plural "-s" is WORSE than random cuts (-2.17 against a null maximum of -1.22): confidence tells clitics from morphology (F5 not hit). The apostrophe-less variant "nt" is beyond the null on informal text and not on edited (rank 0.010 against 0.317); the other variants do not show it.
- Post-hoc (not pre-registered): the confidence ordering has Spearman 0.61 with the treebank effects (n = 7), disagreeing on 'll.
## Limits
One tagger, one run, two English registers, a unigram code; confidence is a proxy for surprise, not a claim a cut is linguistically right; the edited-side apostrophe columns are empty (ERRATUM-1); no creole or non-English text was used.

## The mind (EXPLORATORY; PREREG-MIND.md 618450e4c13a8f36, ADDENDUM-1 d0ad225f57a37a00, ERRATUM-MIND-1 c641e143ddb28b66, ERRATUM-MIND-2 dc49518ef62105cd, ADDENDUM-2 a836cd5c647b5e5b)
Wired in: `readingRows(g, text, { mind })` (khora, adapters/text/mind-cuts.js) applies learned word-final cut programs; the pharos fold `trails` reads the ants' deposits back; khora's kernel/stigmergy.js orders the search. Runs, in the order they happened (all files kept):
- `mind-result-a.json`: DEGENERATE (the stopping rule quit after one failed try; ERRATUM-MIND-1). No conclusion drawn.
- `mind-result-b.json`: showed that "beats a random-cut null" is not a sufficient test: 589 programs together RAISED surprise (a program worse than leaving the word glued could still beat the null; ERRATUM-MIND-2). Run `c` was stopped unread.
- `mind-result-b2.json` (protocol order, seed 20261120) and `mind-result-c2.json` (reversed order, seed 20261121), corrected protocol. `mind-result-b3.json` reruns b2 with the H6 bug fixed and the search-aware ceiling added; its curve is identical to b2's.
Findings (one source mix, one tagger):
- H1 not falsified: surprise on the unseen-source probe (ubuntu IRC) -0.615 (b2) and -0.408 (c2) against no mind; on held-out cosem+nus -0.932 and -0.907; every one of 10 random minds of the same size is worse than no mind (+0.64 to +4.9).
- H2 FALSIFIED: learning cost per batch does not fall (b2 324 evaluations over the first three batches and 324 over the last three; c2 121 and 421).
- H3a held (a reader given only the mind rebuilt from the trails reproduced the probe surprise exactly); H3b hit (1.34% and 1.38% of the ingested bytes, target 1%); H3c hit by the pre-registered top-5 measure (0.00; all-program Jaccard 0.365, with a shared core: 's, 'll, 're, ".i", laughter and shouting fragments).
- H4: transfer to an unseen source is positive; the informal mind RAISES surprise on edited text (+0.19, +0.40): a mind has to be kept and applied per source.
- H5 not falsified: the clitics 's, n't, 're, 'll (and 've in b2) are found unprompted; no plain morphology.
- H6 (corrected, b3): finding 50% of the good programs costs 166 evaluations with the trails' order, 336 cold, 614 random (mean); finding 90% costs 1129 warm, 715 cold, 1055 random; all of them 1176, 1069, 1166. The trails speed the strong half and not the tail (F6 hit at 90%).
- H7 (b3): the search-aware ceiling (largest positive change any candidate reaches on a token-internal character-permuted copy) is 4.825; of 63 good programs only 3 pass it (not, ER, n't); of the clitics only n't. Garbage tokens swing tagger confidence hard, so this bar may be too harsh as well as the per-program bar too weak: both are measurement problems to fix before any claim.
- Worry kept open: about half of each mind is single uppercase letters, and c2 learned dates and accented characters (content fragments); a per-candidate bar of 1 in 31 over ~1000 candidates admits ~30 false positives a batch.

## Calibration (EXPLORATORY; PREREG-CALIBRATION f6e44c4e3e4b1ff5, ADDENDUM-1 84c4e3f27e501c04, PREREG-CLITIC-CTX 307a36cd1559261c)
Files: `calibrate.mjs`, `calibration-result-run1.json` (all-token measure), `calibration-result-ctx.json` (context-only), `clitic-ctx.mjs`, `clitic-ctx-result.json`. Planted-truth text (noise = token-internal character shuffle; five derived function words glued onto their predecessor; a rare planted suffix) with every candidate evaluated.
- The all-token confidence bar is MISCALIBRATED: on pure noise it called 16 to 29% of candidates good per batch (theory at most 3.2%) and 20% good in two independent batches (theory 0.10%): the false positives are systematic. Cause (diagnosis, then tested): the new PIECES carry the change (a lone capital letter is a confidently tagged token), and the null is not matched on the piece. Replication across batches did NOT fix it (non-planted 99 replicated vs 150 once).
- The CONTEXT-ONLY measure (the cut token and its pieces excluded) is calibrated on noise: 4.0 to 5.9% good per batch (the bar of at most 6.5% held) and 0.89% replicated; replication now removes 20 times (5 replicated vs 102 once). But planted-word recall collapsed: only `the` in two or more batches (1 of 5, against 5 of 5 all-token): either the measure is blind to local repairs (the repair lives in the cut token's own pieces) or 40 sampled sentences is too few (to be tested).
- Re-check of the clitic result under the context-only measure (200 sentences, 100 null draws, informal): n't +0.941 (null max 0.379), 've +0.357, 'll +0.276, 'd +0.257, 're +0.167 all beyond the null (0 of 100); 'm +0.156 (1 of 100 reach it), 's +0.130 (3 of 100); plural s -0.256 within the null (12 of 100). 5 of 7 clitics beyond the null (P1 held at its threshold); Spearman with the treebank effects 0.18 against 0.61 for the all-token ordering (F3 hit, post-hoc, n = 7): the tagger's context confidence misses what 's does for the parse.
- Consequence: the earlier all-token statement "all seven clitics beyond the null" is NOT withdrawn for n't, 've, 're, 'd, 'll and is weakened for 's and 'm; and the mind runs (mind-result-*.json) used the all-token measure, so their program sets must be read with this miscalibration in mind (a mind rerun with the context-only measure and replication is the next test).

### Power test (PREREG-CALIBRATION-ADDENDUM-2 304a80eb1cff4bf9; `calibration-result-power.json`)
Context-only measure, 200 sampled sentences, four batches. Planted function words good in two or more batches: the 3, can 2, for 2, you 1, and 1 = 3 of 5 (up from 1 of 5 at 40 sentences): INCONCLUSIVE by the pre-registered rule (4 to pass, fewer than 3 to fail), but the measure is not blind to local repairs; it was underpowered. FP2 HIT: on pure noise (150 random candidates per batch) the good rate rose to 11.3 to 19.6% per batch at 200 sentences (4.0 to 5.9% at 40): the larger sample resolves REAL small effects, because cutting some characters off garbage genuinely changes the tagger's confidence in the neighbouring tokens. A generic random-cut null therefore cannot calibrate this signal at any sample size; a null matched to the same SUFFIX (the same cut on a character-shuffled copy of the same text, subtracted) is the next thing to test. (The replicated count in this run is not meaningful: each batch evaluated a different random subset of candidates.)

### CORRECTION (2026-11-20, after the codec session's independent check): the context-only tagger measure is NOT a calibrated signal
The codec session ran my check independently (experiments/ants/confcheck.mjs in janus, DEV, 455 word-final candidates, 1000 sentences, 30 null cuts): FULL measure 54 of 410 good on real text (13.2%) and 32 of 206 on NOISE (15.5%); context-only 30 of 410 (7.3%) and 26 of 206 on noise (12.6%); chance at most 3.2%. Their context-only noise rate does not reproduce my 4.0 to 5.9% at 40 sampled sentences, and my own power test gave 11 to 20% at 200 sentences. So my statement above that the context-only measure "is calibrated on noise" holds only at that one small sample size and must NOT be relied on; the likely residue is that the tagger's previous-tag features still shift the context tokens' margins. Consequence: tagger margin of any kind is retired from JUDGING programs (PREREG-HELDOUT: tier 1 predictive compression from our own tokens proposes, tier 2 surprise in our own EOT judges). The clitic re-check numbers above (context-only) are kept as a measurement, not as evidence that the clitics beat a calibrated bar.

## Tier 1: held-out predictive compression from our own tokens (EXPLORATORY; PREREG-HELDOUT 666d063e2c482a68; `heldout.mjs`, `heldout-result-calibrate.json`, `heldout-result-real.json`, `heldout-calibrate.out`, `heldout-real.out`)
No tagger or parser: a Witten-Bell token bigram over a character-bigram spelling model, trained only on our own ingested tokens; bits per character of held-out text with a word-final cut applied to training and held-out alike; GOOD on a fold = none of 1000 paired bootstrap resamples (over sentences) of the mean per-sentence change is >= 0; folds are leave-one-batch-out; REPLICATED = good on at least two folds.
- PA HELD (calibrated on noise): good per fold 1.52, 0.44, 0.79, 0.39% of 5186 candidates (theory at most 3.2%, ceiling 6.5%); replicated 0.27% (failure line 0.52%).
- PB HELD: all five derived planted function words good on 3 or 4 of 4 folds (the 4, can 3, you 4, for 4, and 4; change -0.004 to -0.016 bits per character). The replicated non-planted candidates in B (IAO, KKK, KKKK, OMG, day, hehe, ing) are informal-specific patterns found with no list.
- PC HELD: the one-batch rare suffix `zq` was good in 0 folds (no content leakage).
- PD1 FALSIFIED: on the real informal text (8 folds, ~106k tokens) n't is good on 0 of 8 folds and 's on 1 of 8; the mean change for the clitics is -0.00001 to -0.00146 bits per character, inside the bootstrap noise.
- PD2 FALSIFIED: no morphology reaches six folds (-ing 3 of 8, -s, -ed, -ly 0 of 8, -er positive).
Reading: tier 1 is a calibrated, sensitive PROPOSER of statistical-pooling cuts and informal patterns, but predictive compression of tokens does not reward clitic splitting at this data size, consistent with the earlier unigram code-length result: it rewards pooling, not syntactic units. The clitics matter at the level of RELATIONS (n't flips a claim's polarity, 's is the genitive relation), which a token model cannot see: that is tier 2 (surprise in our own EOT), not yet built.
