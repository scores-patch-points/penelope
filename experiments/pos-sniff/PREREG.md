# PREREG — the ablation sniff against khora's tagger, on informal text (written 2026-11-20 BEFORE the first run)

Split label: **REUSED / EXPLORATORY**. The files below were already scored whole (all three files per language) by Zenodotus GL-UD-13 (`sniff-creole.mjs`), so nothing here is fresh or held-out in the register's sense; the model (`organs/data/pos-sniff-eng2.json`, Zenodotus `sniff-eng2.json`, learned from UD English-EWT, CC BY-SA 4.0, embedder nomic-embed-text) is applied FROZEN and was never trained on these files.

Data (read from scores-patch-points/Zenodotus @ cc0894c, MIT / UD licences as in its PROVENANCE): ALL files of each directory, as GL-UD-13 read them (the original harness used every file, nothing is trained on them): Naija Pidgin `pcm_nsc-ud-{train,dev,test}_sample.conllu`, Singlish `pos_singlish/{train_sample,dev,test}.conll`. Occurrences built exactly as GL-UD-13: words of 3 or more letters a-z, nominal = NOUN/PROPN, other = VERB ADJ ADV AUX NUM PRON ADP DET CCONJ PART SCONJ, sentences of 6 to 40 tokens, at most 3 occurrences per word. The sentence text is the gold tokens joined by one space (offsets are exact); no regex or word list reads any text.

Claims, each with its falsifier:
- **P1 fidelity.** The port (reader offsets instead of the original regex) reproduces the original frozen Sniff@2 accuracy within 1.0 point: Naija 0.731 (640 occurrences), Singlish 0.780 (1506). Falsified by a gap above 1 point or different occurrence counts: then the port is not the sniff and nothing below counts.
- **P2 sniff versus tagger.** On the same occurrences, the sniff's nominal-vs-other accuracy beats the khora tagger's (the tagger run on each whole gold-tokenized sentence, its UPOS mapped to the same two classes) in at least 190 of 200 paired bootstrap resamples, in EACH language. Falsified by the tagger matching or beating it in either language: then "much better POS" is not shown on informal text, and that is the finding.
- **P3 where it helps.** Reported, not claimed: accuracy of each by tagger margin third (is the sniff stronger exactly where the tagger is unsure), and the accuracy of the rule "use the sniff only when the tagger's margin is in its bottom third".
- **P4 card.** From the frozen run, the calibration card for the layer "pos-sniff-nominal" (planted = true nominals, hit = the sniff says nominal; noise = true others, hit = the sniff says nominal) is derived from counts by `calibrationCard`, per language; the ledger quotes its likelihood ratio with this stamp.

## ADDENDUM-1 (written AFTER the first run: post-hoc, exploratory; the first run's results stand as recorded)
First run (all occurrences): P1 holds (Naija 0.7312, Singlish 0.7795; original 0.731 and 0.780; same occurrence counts 640 and 1506). **P2 is FALSIFIED**: the khora tagger scores 0.8359 and 0.8459, the sniff wins 0 of 200 resamples in each. P3: the sniff only matches the tagger in the tagger's lowest-margin third on Singlish (0.685 against 0.677) and is worse everywhere on Naija; the hybrid (sniff where the tagger's margin is lowest) is 0.8486 against 0.8459 on Singlish and worse on Naija (0.8063 against 0.8359).
The sniff was DESIGNED for words the prior does not know (`isUnknownWord`), and the first run scored every word, including common ones the tagger has seen. Addendum question, asked after seeing this: on the occurrences whose word the tagger's lexicon LACKS (its prior's `lex.has(lower-cased form)` is false), how do the two compare? Reported with n, accuracies and the same paired bootstrap, labelled POST-HOC. No bar is set after the fact; a win here would be a hypothesis for a fresh set, not a result.

**ERRATUM-1 to the addendum (found on the first addendum run, before any addendum number was read):** the gate `lex.has(...)` was wrong: `parser.lex` is null (the English tagger has no UniMorph feature loaded, the reader runs it the same way) and every occurrence was counted unknown (n equal to all 640 and 1506). The tagger's prior of known forms is `parser.lexicon.form`, whose keys are `form|UPOS` from the training treebank; a form is known when any key carries it. The addendum now uses that.
