Reading, correcting my F1 summary: "claims that state their own time are trustworthy falsifiers" does NOT generalise from states and capitals to administrative divisions. A stated year is necessary but not sufficient. The dev/F2 true-pair disputes with stated years are sub-entity numbers: Groningen 231,299 (2016, the city) vs 601,000 (the province); Limburg 120,837 vs 1,138,000; Zeeland 54,589 vs 395,000; Meghalaya 3.2M vs 143,000 (Shillong); Nagaland 1.98M vs 16,527 (a town); Delhi 11.0M vs 16.8M (city vs the territory, same year). Provinces name their capital and its population beside the province's own, and the number nearest the keyword can be either. Also, same-category impostors (provinces with similar populations) pass a number test by coincidence (agree 13% in de), so a number alone is a weak identifier, unlike a birth year.
Consequence: only a claim with a stated time AND a verified tie to the subject (which entity the number describes) can falsify. The organ should carry a claim's subject-tie as a mark beside its time. A quantity is a weak falsifier among similar impostors; an identity-bearing invariant (birth year) is a strong one.
Limits: administrative divisions only; fr/de only; regex extractor; the interval-based reading of falsifier 1 was chosen before the run (ADDENDUM-1).
