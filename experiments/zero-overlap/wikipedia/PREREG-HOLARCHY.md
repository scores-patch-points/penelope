# PREREG-HOLARCHY: does the dependency order survive translation without shared names? (filed before it was run). EXPLORATORY: F1-F4 are already read; this is a first real-data look, not a held-out claim.
Idea (Wilber's holarchy, the destroy test): a region is a whole whose parts are its provinces; destroy the region and all its provinces fall. If two articles describe the same set of things, the GROUPING of provinces under regions must be the same partition in both languages, even though the region names differ (Toscana / Tuscany / Toscane) and no word is shared. This tests a relationship structure, not a fact or a number.
Extraction (regex, no model, nothing learned): the CONTAINER of an article = the capitalised name next to the word region in its lead: en "region of X", "the X region"; fr "région (de|du|de la|d')X", "située en X"; de "Region X". Names are normalised (accents stripped, lower-case) and compared only WITHIN a language, so no cross-language name matching is needed.
Measure: for each category (scene) with at least 8 members that have a container on both sides, take every pair of members (i, j): sameEN = they share the English container; sameOT = they share the other-language container. Report the agreement rate (sameEN == sameOT), P(sameOT | sameEN) and P(sameOT | not sameEN), and compare with a SHUFFLE null: the other-language container labels permuted among the same members (500 shuffles, seed 20261118), which keeps each language's own partition and breaks the pairing. Also the destroy-set agreement: for each English container, the best Jaccard overlap of its member set with some other-language container's member set (weighted by size), against the same shuffle null.
Falsifier (in advance): if the observed destroy-set agreement is not above the 95th percentile of the shuffle null, or P(sameOT | sameEN) is not at least 0.8 with a lift above 2 over P(sameOT | not sameEN), the dependency order does not survive translation by this extraction (or the extraction is too noisy to show it).
Not claimed: anything about hierarchies deeper than one level; names that are not regions (counties, departments); a held-out. Coverage is reported.

## ADDENDUM-1: from the partition to a pair verdict (filed before it was run). Still EXPLORATORY (F1-F4, already read).
Question: given that the grouping carries across languages, what can it say about ONE candidate pair (English article p, other-language article of member k)? A partition cannot tell two provinces of the same region apart; it can say whether the candidate is in the right region.
Definition (nothing hand-set): for the members of one scene that have a container on both sides, let RE(p) = {p} plus the members sharing p's English container, and RO(k) = {k} plus the members sharing k's other-language container. J(p, k) = |RE(p) ∩ RO(k)| / |RE(p) ∪ RO(k)|. M(p) = the set of candidates k with the highest J(p, k) (all ties kept). Verdict for (p, k): SAME-REGION if k is in M(p); OTHER if not (default Other: a candidate that is not among the best-matching region-sets is rejected). The truth (k = p) is never used to build M(p).
Reported per language: RECALL = the share of p whose true counterpart is in M(p) (a true pair called OTHER is a miss); NARROWING = mean |M(p)| / n against the scene size n (a smaller share means more impostors rejected); REJECTION of impostors = 1 - (|M(p)| - 1)/(n - 1); all against a SHUFFLE null in which the other-language container labels are permuted among the members (200 shuffles, seed 20261118), which keeps each language's own partition and breaks the pairing.
Falsifier (in advance): recall below 0.80, or impostor rejection not above the null's 95th percentile, means the partition is not a usable region verdict by this extraction.
Not claimed: a verdict that separates two members of one region (impossible by construction); anything on pairs without a container (they are gaps, reported with coverage).

## ADDENDUM-2: chaining the region verdict with area (filed before it was run). EXPLORATORY on F1-F4 (already read).
Chain: for a claimant p in a scene, M(p) = the best-matching region set (ADDENDUM-1, computed over members with a container on both sides). Candidate k is judged: OTHER if k is not in M(p) OR the areas dispute (> 0.25 apart); SAME if k is in M(p) AND the areas agree (<= 0.10 apart, the frozen area extractor and thresholds of PREREG-CORROB); GAP otherwise (area missing or unclear). Evaluated on claimants whose English and other-language areas and containers are all present; candidates are the scene members that have an other-language area and a container.
Reported: TP (true counterpart SAME), FA (scene-mate SAME), gap rate; the same members judged by area alone (SAME iff the areas agree) for comparison; Wilson 95% intervals; fr and de separately.
Falsifier (in advance): the chain's false-accept rate must be below the area-alone false-accept rate with a non-overlapping interval, and its TP not below the area-alone TP by more than 0.05. If not, region adds nothing to area here (or the sample is too small to show it, reported as such).
Not claimed: anything on pairs without both a container and an area (gap); a held-out.

## ADDENDUM-3 (POST-HOC, filed after seeing ADDENDUM-2's result, before this run; exploratory iteration on the same data, so only a hint)
ADDENDUM-2 result: the chain cut the false accept about 20x (de 0.123 to 0.006) but lost true pairs (TP 0.659 vs 1.000), because a noisy region misses the true counterpart. Variant "area first, region breaks ties": A(p) = the candidates whose areas agree (<= 0.10); if |A| = 1 that candidate is the answer; if |A| > 1 the answer is A intersected with M(p) when that is non-empty, else A. Reported: truth kept in the answer set, mean answer-set size, and unique-and-correct (size 1 and the truth), against area alone (answer set = A). Not a verdict rule I would trust until a fresh set confirms it.
