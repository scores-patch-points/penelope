## Soft chain on a FRESH set F5 (PREREG-SOFTCHAIN sha 53b71173a766ce09). holarchy4.py, fetch_fresh5.py; softchain-f5-fr.log, softchain-f5-de.log.
F5: 16 categories (States of Nigeria, Regions of Tanzania, Provinces of Bulgaria, Ecuador, Madagascar, the DR Congo, Papua New Guinea, Counties of Sweden, Provinces of China, Governorates of Iraq, Districts of Portugal, Counties of Estonia, Regions of the Czech Republic, Provinces of Laos, Departments of Honduras, Regions of Namibia); fr 290 / de 307 pairs, fetched unread, run once.
RESULT: ELIGIBLE CLAIMANTS 0 in both languages: no category has 8 or more members whose leads name a region on both sides (a container on both sides for only 14 fr and 4 de pairs; an English container for 35 of each). Falsifier (4) (fewer than 30 eligible claimants) HIT. The soft chain is NOT confirmed and NOT refuted. Areas are not the limit: about a third of F5 English leads state an area (93 of 290, 99 of 307).
Why: the container reader is a regex built around the word "region" (en "region of X", fr "region de X" / "situee en X", de "Region X"). It fired for Italian provinces, Japanese prefectures and a few others; the leads of African, Asian and Latin American divisions, and of Swedish, Estonian or Portuguese ones, mostly name no region. The hierarchy is in the infobox and category structure more than in the lead sentence the extractor reads.
Reading: the region layer works where the lead names its region and is silent elsewhere, so the earlier German result (n = 41, Italy and Japan) stays an unconfirmed hint. To test the idea on new data one needs a container reader that does not depend on the word "region": the infobox parent, Wikipedia's category or link structure, or khora's reader.
