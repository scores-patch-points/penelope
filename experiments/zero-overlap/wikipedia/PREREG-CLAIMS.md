# PREREG-CLAIMS: invariant claims from Wikipedia leads, compared with compareClaims (filed before scoring)
Claim extraction (rules, no model, nothing learned): from the first parenthesis in the first 400 characters of a lead, take the 4-digit years 1000-2029 in order. Two or more years -> born_year = first, died_year = second (invariants, kind "invariant"); exactly one year preceded within the parenthesis by born / né / née / geboren / * -> born_year only. Frame = "calendar-year, lead-section". Subject: the candidate pairing asserts both articles are the same subject, so both sides carry subject "X"; the comparison is the falsification attempt of that assertion (your definition).
Comparison: compareClaims. A pair is FALSIFIED if any invariant disputes; SURVIVES if >= 1 invariant agrees and none dispute; NO CLAIMS otherwise (a hole, not a vote).
Arms: true pairs (the article and its translation) and suitor pairs (the article against the other-language lead of each scene-mate).
Splits: rules written looking at 8 sample leads only. DEV = acts <= 3: I read every dispute on true pairs by hand and fix extraction bugs, listed in an erratum. Then the rules are FROZEN and run ONCE on acts >= 4. Disclosure: that held-out was used by earlier, different frames; this frame learns nothing from it and is run once after freeze.
Reported: coverage (share of pairs where both sides yield >= 1 claim), true-pair survive / falsified, suitor falsified / survive (coincidence), by split and language (fr, de). Not claimed: anything about pairs without lifespans (most of the set), fluents (population, office: no extraction yet), or an adversary that rewrites the lead.
Falsifiers (in advance): (1) the true-pair falsified rate on covered pairs, after the dev read-through, > 0.05 on held-out: extraction or the frame is unreliable. (2) the suitor-falsified rate on covered pairs < 0.80: the claims frame has weak power to reject impostors. (3) coverage < 0.05: too narrow to matter (reported as a limit either way).
Adversary note: an adversary that only appends tokens cannot change the first parenthesis; one that rewrites the lead can forge a fake biography. That attack is fabrication, not impersonation, and is listed as a gap.

## ERRATUM-1 (after the dev read-through, before any held-out run). Rules FROZEN as below.
Dev: true-pair falsified 1/60 (fr) and 6/172 (de). I read all 7; every one is an EXTRACTION bug, not a real disagreement: (1) the first parenthesis was not the life-span parenthesis (a de alias "deutsch 1920-1934 auch ..."; an fr lead with an unrelated "(1876...)"); (2) long parentheses (Russian transliterations) exceeded the 200-character window so a later short parenthesis (the death year alone) was read; (3) a lone year after a death marker was taken as a birth.
Fixes (frozen): choose the first parenthesis, starting within the first 600 characters and up to 400 characters long, that has a date-like pattern (a day number next to a word and a 4-digit year, or a * or dagger marker) with a 4-digit year; if it has * or dagger markers, born_year = the first year after *, died_year = the first year after the dagger; otherwise the first two years (born, died) or a single year right after born / ne / nee / geboren. 
ERRATUM-1b (still dev, before held-out): the remaining 5 de disputes were one more extraction bug: de leads print Julian/Gregorian tags glued to the year ("1895greg."), so the year regex with a word boundary missed the birth year and the next year (the death year) was taken. Year pattern changed to a digit-boundary match (not preceded or followed by a digit). Rules are FROZEN after this change.
