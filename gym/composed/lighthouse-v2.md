# The Compass Light

## Scene 1

The storm had passed, leaving behind a melancholy stillness that clung to the lighthouse like a damp shroud. Mara stood at the gallery, her eyes scanning the turbulent sea as the sun dipped towards the horizon. The waves crashed against the rocky shore, a constant reminder of her isolation. One year ago tonight, Tomas had vanished into the darkness, leaving behind only the faint hum of his compass and the echo of his laughter.

Mara's gaze wandered to the brass compass that sat in the drawer of her dresser, its golden surface gleaming dully in the fading light. She hadn't dared open it since the night he disappeared, as if some hidden truth lay locked within its mechanism. The logbook she now held, however, told a different story. A weathered leather cover and yellowed pages spoke of a sailor's journey, one that had ended in disaster on this very shore.

As she walked down to the beach, the logbook cradled in her hands, Mara felt an eerie sense of connection to the stranger who had left behind his tale. The sand was cool beneath her feet, and the sea air carried the scent of salt and decay. She unfolded the pages, her eyes scanning the scribbled entries as the sun dipped below the horizon. A single sentence caught her attention: "Storm raged in, darkness closed in." Mara's heart quickened, sensing a kinship with this sailor who had also faced the tempests of fate on that fateful night. As she read on, her gaze drifted towards the surf, where something pale and unrecognizable broke the surface, sending a shiver down her spine.

## Scene 2

As the waves lapped against the shore, Mara waded into the foam, her eyes scanning the scattered detritus of the sea. Her fingers danced across the sand, searching for something that would tell her a story. She spotted it then – a weathered logbook, half-buried in the shallows. The cover was water-stained and cracked, but as she grasped it, she sensed a connection to Tomas's missing world.

With a gentle pull, Mara extricated the book from the sand. Water dripped from its pages like tears, carrying with it the whispers of secrets past. She opened the worn leather, and the musty scent wafted up, heavy with memories. The entries were dated close to the night Tomas vanished – a mere fortnight after he'd set sail.

Mara's fingers trailed along the pages, tracing the sailor's handwriting as the story unfolded. The words spoke of calm seas and fair winds, but beneath the surface, a sense of foreboding seethed. A shipwreck loomed on the horizon, its fate sealed by the cruel hand of Storm. Mara felt a shiver run down her spine as she read, "Storm raged in, darkness closed in." She'd heard it before – in Tomas's own words, echoing from the pages of this very logbook.

As she delved deeper into the book, the writing grew more erratic, the sentences disjointed. The sailor's grip on reality began to slip, and Mara sensed a kinship with him, too – a shared understanding that some storms could consume you whole. She closed her eyes, letting the words wash over her, and when she opened them again, the sky was darkening, as if Storm itself had come for her.

## Scene 3

Mara's fingers trailed over the pages of Elian Voss's logbook, the worn leather creaking in protest as if afraid to reveal its secrets any further. The handwriting, once neat and precise, had given way to scribbles and wild doodles, like a shipwrecked sailor clinging to sanity by threads of madness. She paused on a particular entry, her eyes locking onto the phrase "hear the horn in silence." A shiver ran down her spine as she realized that it was an echo of her own experiences – nights when the foghorn's mournful wail seemed to pierce the darkness, even when its light was extinguished. It was a sound she'd never spoken of before, not even to her closest friends on the station.

As she delved deeper into the logbook, Mara discovered that Elian Voss had heard it too – in moments of isolation, when the world seemed to hold its breath. The entries grew increasingly fragmented, like shards of glass reflecting moonlight on a stormy sea. She read of storms brewing inside him, as if they'd somehow taken residence within his mind, driving him to scribble down words that made little sense to anyone but himself. Mara felt a strange connection to the sailor's despair, a sense of solidarity with someone who knew what it was like to be lost at sea – or in this case, lost in the silence of her own mind. The logbook seemed to hold secrets she was desperate to uncover, and as she read on, the darkness closing in around her grew thicker, like the mist that shrouded the island.

## Scene 4

The dim light of dawn seeped through the narrow window, casting an eerie glow on the pages of the logbook as Mara turned them with increasing urgency. The cramped handwriting seemed to dance across the parchment, a desperate attempt to leave behind a trail of hope for someone – or something. She felt like she was unraveling a puzzle, each word and symbol drawing her closer to the truth.

A tattered page fluttered open, its edges worn from exposure to the elements. Mara's eyes scanned the scribbled coordinates, her heart racing with anticipation. Tomas's compass lay abandoned on the floor, its brass surface gleaming like a beacon in the morning light. She felt an inexplicable connection to the instrument, as if it held the secrets she sought.

The handwriting on the page was shaky, the letters blurring into each other – 42° 27' N, 122° 11' W – but Mara's mind was determined to make sense of it all. This could be it, the key to finding Tomas's boat and unraveling the mystery that had haunted her for so long. The possibilities swirled in her head like the fog that shrouded the island, thick and impenetrable.

She felt a shiver run down her spine as she read on, the words blurring together into an unintelligible mess. But then she saw it – a single sentence scrawled across the page in bold letters: "I have lost all sense of time." The words seemed to leap off the page, echoing through Mara's mind like a haunting warning.

## Scene 5

Mara's fingers trailed across the yellowed pages of the logbook, her eyes scanning the cramped handwriting with a mix of nostalgia and trepidation. She had always felt a sense of duty towards Tomas's boat, now a hulking wreck on the shore, its mast splintered and its hull cracked open like a skeleton. The light she tended, that beacon of safety in the fog, seemed to mock her now – a cruel reminder of all she'd lost.

As she read on, Mara's doubts intensified. What right did she still have to care for the light? Grief had hollowed out the purpose of its existence, leaving only a hollow shell of what it once was. She wondered if someone else should take up the post – perhaps Elian Voss, the stranger who'd claimed Tomas's logbook as his own. There was something unsettling about that, too – the way Elian's name seemed to echo through Mara's mind like a whispered promise.

Mara turned her attention to a page she hadn't seen before, one that had been tucked away at the back of the book. The handwriting on this page was shaky and illegible, as if Tomas had been in a state of panic when he'd written it. A set of coordinates, torn from the page with a jagged edge, caught her eye – they seemed to pulse with a strange energy, like they were trying to draw her towards them. Mara's mind wandered back to the spot where Tomas's boat had gone down, the place marked by that torn page in Elian's own hand. Was it the same place? And if so, what secrets lay hidden beneath the waves?

## Scene 6

The darkness seeped into Mara's bones like a chill, each breath a reminder that the night was almost upon her. She stood at the edge of the lighthouse, the wind whispers secrets in her ear, and yet she couldn't shake the feeling that she was being watched. The foghorn, usually silenced for months, had been activated once before, on this very anniversary, leaving Mara with a sense of disquiet that lingered long after the sound faded.

Tonight felt different, though. The air was heavier, like the weight of memories accumulated over years. Mara's thoughts drifted to Tomas, and the events that led to his disappearance – the storm, the shipwreck, the coordinates scrawled in Elian's logbook. She had kept the light burning for him, but grief had stolen its meaning, leaving her wondering if she still had a right to tend it.

The lighthouse's wooden beams creaked softly in response to her shifting weight, as if responding to some unheard question. Mara felt the familiar tug of her compass, Tomas's trusty brass instrument, and for an instant, she was back on the boat with him, sailing into the unknown. But that moment vanished like a mirage, leaving her with only the oppressive silence. The wind picked up, rustling the fog, and Mara knew it was time to take her place at the lantern room. She straightened her shoulders, shouldering the weight of the light once more, as the first wisps of dawn began to creep over the horizon.

## Scene 7

As she descended into the lantern room, the silence was oppressive, a weight that pressed upon her chest like the darkness itself. Mara lit the wick, watching as the flame danced to life within the glass sphere, casting an eerie glow over the fog-shrouded sea. She began her rounds, checking the gauges and adjusting the valves with practiced ease.

At the very top of the lantern room, in a corner where the light struggled to penetrate, she paused, her hand resting on the brass compass that had once belonged to Tomas. The instrument lay still, its needle quivering only slightly as Mara's fingers made contact. It was then she heard it – the foghorn's mournful bellow, low and haunting.

But this time, something felt different. As she listened, a shiver ran down her spine, and for an instant, she wondered if she was truly alone in the lantern room. The sound seemed... wrong. Not quite like anything she'd ever heard before. A slight tremor ran through the metal as Mara turned to face the controls, her eyes scanning the gauges with a newfound intensity.

It hit her then – a realization that made her breath catch and her heart stumble. The foghorn was never supposed to sound on nights when it was switched off. And yet, here she was, listening to its mournful wail as if she'd grown accustomed to it. A slow understanding dawned upon her: the sound wasn't just from the lantern room – it came from within herself, a haunting echo of memories long buried and forgotten.

## Scene 8

Mara's fingers trembled as she delicately unfolded the logbook, the yellowed pages crackling with age. She had spent countless hours poring over its contents, searching for answers about Tomas's fate and her own connection to him. A faint scent of saltwater and tar wafted up from the pages, transporting her back to the stormy night she'd first heard the foghorn.

Her eyes scanned the worn leather binding, searching for the familiar symbols that marked the beginning of each entry. The torn page lay hidden near the end, its corner folded under like a crumpled wing. Mara's heart quickened as she spotted the faint outline of handwriting on the edge – shaking hand, just like the one who had scribbled down those cryptic coordinates.

She delicately pried open the cover, her breath catching at the sight of the ink-stained page. The writing was erratic, barely legible, but Mara's trained eye picked out the numbers and letters that made up the set of coordinates – 43° 27' N, 123° 42' W. She felt a jolt of excitement mixed with trepidation as she realized this might be it – the location where Tomas's boat had gone down, maybe even where the ship itself had met its demise.

A shiver ran down Mara's spine as she tucked the logbook away, her mind racing with questions and possibilities. She knew, without a doubt, that she had to try and find these coordinates – for herself, for Tomas, and for the truth about what had happened all those years ago.

## Scene 9

Mara's eyes wandered back to the logbook, her gaze tracing the yellowed pages with a mixture of nostalgia and longing. The more she read, the more she felt the weight of Tomas's disappearance settling upon her like a shroud. She had to know what had happened out there – on those treacherous waters.

As she turned the final page, Mara made up her mind. She would set sail herself, alone and unaccompanied. The thought sent a thrill through her veins, followed by a pang of doubt. Was she ready for this? Had she been foolish to think she could do what Tomas had tried and failed to do?

But as she stood there, the compass on her workbench seemed to stir from its slumber, its brass surface glinting in the fading light like a promise. Mara felt an inexplicable connection to the instrument, a sense that it was urging her on. She took the logbook back, running her fingers over the pages once more.

This had to be done. The season was already turning, and she couldn't wait any longer. Mara made her decision: she would sail out to those coordinates tomorrow, no matter what. She wouldn't waste another day wondering or hesitating. The sea was waiting for her, its secrets whispering in the darkness. With a sense of determination that surprised her, Mara tore off the lid from the nearby lantern and lit it, casting a warm glow over the cluttered room.

## Scene 10

The flickering lantern cast eerie shadows on the walls as Mara's eyes roamed over the cluttered room. She'd lit it, just like she had so many times before, but tonight felt different. The light itself seemed to hum with a sorrow that echoed her own. It was a bittersweet reminder of all that she'd lost. 

Grief had hollowed out the work of its meaning, leaving Mara uncertain about her place here. She thought of the countless nights she'd spent tending this very light, guiding ships through the treacherous waters with a steady hand and unwavering heart. But was she still worthy? Did she have the right to hold that responsibility? The more she considered it, the more doubts crept in.

Mara's gaze drifted back to the logbook on her workbench, its pages filled with the meticulous records of every passage, every storm, and every triumph. It had been her lifeline, a connection to the world beyond this small, isolated outpost. But as she read over the entries, each word seemed hollow now. The stories were no longer hers to tell.

She rose from her chair, the lantern's warm glow casting an otherworldly light on her face. Mara took a step forward, then another, until she stood at the door, gazing out into the darkness. The sea was waiting for her, but what did it want from her now? She couldn't shake the feeling that she was no longer needed here. The thought sent a shiver down her spine, and Mara knew she had to make a decision.

## Scene 11

Mara stood at the door, the lantern's light dancing on the waves outside, as if beckoning her out to sea. The silence was oppressive, punctuated only by the occasional hoot of a gull or the creaking of ancient timbers beneath her feet. She felt the weight of the house behind her, its secrets and stories pressing in like the tide.

The anniversary loomed closer, its presence a palpable thing, like the scent of decay on the wind. Mara's thoughts were torn between the memories that lingered here – the laughter of children, the warmth of firesides – and the darkness that had driven her to this moment.

A faint scratching at the door broke the stillness, and Mara turned to see Kael, the keeper, standing in the entrance hall, his face a map of creases and lines etched by years of watching over the house. His eyes met hers, and for an instant, they were suspended in time.

"You're leaving," he said, his voice low and rough, like the sound of stones being turned. Mara's heart skipped a beat as she nodded, the lantern's light casting eerie shadows on her face. Kael's gaze lingered on her before he turned to disappear into the darkness, leaving Mara alone once more with her thoughts and the sea's unyielding call.

## Scene 12

The darkness outside seemed to press in closer as Mara stood by the table, her gaze fixed on the logbook's worn cover. The lantern's light danced across the pages, illuminating the cryptic entries and the coordinates that had drawn her to this place for so long. She felt a restlessness building inside her, like the tides responding to some unseen force.

"Tonight," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the creaking of the old house, "I'll set sail."

She felt a shiver run down her spine as the words left her lips, but it wasn't fear that gripped her. It was something else – a sense of resolve, of necessity. She couldn't shake the feeling that if she waited any longer, the moment would pass, and the sea's call would grow too loud to ignore.

Mara's eyes drifted back to the page, where Voss had written the coordinates in his spidery script: 43.7232° N, 71.2134° W. She felt a surge of determination as she reached for the compass on the table, its brass surface glinting in the lantern light. The needle quivered, as if sensing her intent.

She would sail to those coordinates, alone and unaided. No longer could she rely on Voss's words or the sea itself to guide her. Tonight, she would take control of her own destiny. With a quiet resolve, Mara turned to leave the room, the logbook's secrets locked away for now, but the sea's call echoing in her heart.

## Scene 13

Mara stood at the helm, the wind whipping her hair into a frenzy as she gazed out at the horizon. The sun had long since set, leaving behind a deep indigo that seemed to vibrate with the pulse of the sea. She scanned the waters, her eyes searching for any sign of what lay ahead.

The needle on Tomas's compass wavered, then steadied, pointing due east. Mara's heart quickened as she charted their course, the familiar rhythm of the waves a comforting presence. The logbook's secrets remained locked away, but tonight, she sought closure.

As they sailed, the sea seemed to grow darker, the shadows deepening into something almost palpable. Mara shivered, despite the warmth of the lantern-lit room where she had made her decision. Something was out here with them now, a presence that watched and waited.

And then, like a whispered promise, Mara saw it – a small cluster of debris bobbing on the surface. She squinted, her eyes narrowing as she took in the tangled wreckage. A ship's mast, its wooden slats worn smooth by time and saltwater. A rusting anchor, half-buried in the sand.

A marker, emblazoned with a single word – Voss. Mara's breath caught in her throat as she felt the weight of it settle upon her. She reached out, her fingers closing around the cold metal as if claiming something long lost. The sea seemed to sigh, its waves lapping gently at the shore like a requiem for the dead.

## Scene 14

The sun had long since abandoned its vigil, casting a deep, foreboding shadow across the water. Mara stood at the edge of the wreckage, her fingers still wrapped around the anchor as if it might vanish into thin air if she let go. The sea's sigh seemed to have stilled, leaving an oppressive silence in its wake.

She was not alone. A figure emerged from the darkness, their footsteps sinking into the sand with a deliberate slowness. Mara's grip on the anchor tightened as she sensed the approach of another. The keeper, Kael, had come for her, though he did not know it yet. His presence was a harbinger of things to be said and unspoken.

As Kael drew near, Mara felt the weight of the anniversary bearing down upon her. It was tonight, the night Voss's ship had vanished into the storm that had claimed Tomas's boat as well. The memories came flooding back: the screams, the chaos, the desperation that had driven Voss to chart a course into the unknown.

Mara's mind recoiled from the recollections, and she turned away from Kael, her eyes fixed on the horizon. The sea was calm now, its surface smooth as glass. But Mara knew better than to trust appearances. She felt the presence of What lay beyond the waves, waiting for her, and a growing sense of unease that had nothing to do with the storm or the sea.

In this darkness, time lost all meaning.

## Scene 15

As she followed Kael along the coastline, the sun had dipped below the horizon, casting the sea in a deep indigo hue. The air was heavy with the scent of salt and seaweed, a familiar comfort to Mara's weary senses. She navigated through the rocks, her eyes fixed on the metal compass in Tomas's hand, its brass face glinting faintly as she led them closer to their destination.

A cluster of boulders loomed ahead, worn smooth by the relentless pounding of the waves. Kael halted, his gaze scanning the beach before him. Mara joined him, her heart quickening with anticipation. The compass needle quivered, pointing to a spot just beyond the rocks, where the sea had receded in a shallow pool.

Mara's eyes locked onto the water, and she felt the world slow around her. In the center of the pool, half-submerged and covered in seaweed, was the wooden hull of Voss's ship. The once-sturdy vessel now lay broken and battered, its mast snapped like a brittle twig. A glint caught her attention – a silver marker, embedded in the wooden frame, bearing an inscription that made her breath catch: "We sailed into darkness, and found nothing."

As she took in this confirmation of her research, Mara's gaze drifted to the spot where Tomas's boat had been lost. On the shore, buried beneath a tangle of beach grasses, lay the hull of his vessel – weathered, worn, but unmistakably intact. The compass needle stabilized, its face now pointing directly at the marker. With a quiet sense of resolution, Mara felt the presence of What recede from her awareness, leaving behind only a sense of peace, and closure.
