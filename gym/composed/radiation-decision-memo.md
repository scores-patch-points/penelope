The primary disagreement emerges from the assessment of risk at low-dose ionizing radiation. The ICRP, in its regulatory framework, prioritizes caution and employs a conservative approach based on the Linear No Threshold (LNT) model. (ICRP)(BEIR_VII) This stance is supported by the substantial uncertainty inherent in estimating risks at low doses. (BEIR_VII)  While ICRP acknowledges the limitations of the LNT model and emphasizes the need for further research, it maintains that cautionary measures are warranted due to the potential for unknown biological effects.  Conversely, Brenner's epidemiological perspective advocates for continued research but suggests a reluctance to abandon the LNT model in light of the lack of definitive human evidence for alternative models. (Brenner) 

The BEIR VII report similarly acknowledges uncertainty regarding low-dose risk assessment, but their conclusion leans towards a linear response at low doses. (BEIR_VII) However, they highlight this conclusion's significant uncertainty and recommend continued research before concluding on alternative models.  This nuanced stance underscores the complex nature of analyzing low-dose risks, emphasizing the need for cautious yet scientifically informed decision-making in light of current evidence and ongoing research.

Despite acknowledging the inherent complexities and uncertainties surrounding low-dose radiation risk assessment, a consensus on a definitive safe threshold remains elusive. (ENS)  The ICRP's recommendation of a 1 mSv annual dose limit for the public serves as a foundational benchmark, yet individual experts offer diverse perspectives. (ICRP) While some advocate for a strictly defined threshold (Brenner), others prefer a more flexible framework based on estimated risk levels and continued research (Tubiana). (Tubiana) (Brenner) The uncertainty surrounding low-dose risks necessitates a cautious approach, prioritizing further investigation before definitively adopting an alternative model.  However, the existing evidence suggests that a robust evaluation of both the potential benefits and consequences of exposure to low-dose radiation is essential for informed decision-making in this complex area.
