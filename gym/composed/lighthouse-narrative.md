# The Compass Light

## Scene 1

The storm had passed, leaving behind a melancholy stillness that clung to the lighthouse like a damp shroud. Mara stood at the gallery, her eyes scanning the turbulent sea as the sun dipped towards the horizon. The waves crashed against the rocky shore, a constant reminder of her isolation. One year ago tonight, Tomas had vanished into the darkness, leaving behind only the faint hum of his compass and the echo of his laughter.

Mara's gaze wandered to the brass compass that sat in the drawer of her dresser, its golden surface gleaming dully in the fading light. She hadn't dared open it since the night he disappeared, as if some hidden truth lay locked within its mechanism. The logbook she now held, however, told a different story. A weathered leather cover and yellowed pages spoke of a sailor's journey, one that had ended in disaster on this very shore.

As she walked down to the beach, the logbook cradled in her hands, Mara felt an eerie sense of connection to the stranger who had left behind his tale. The sand was cool beneath her feet, and the sea air carried the scent of salt and decay. She unfolded the pages, her eyes scanning the scribbled entries as the sun dipped below the horizon. A single sentence caught her attention: "Storm raged in, darkness closed in." Mara's heart quickened, sensing a kinship with this sailor who had also faced the tempests of fate on that fateful night. As she read on, her gaze drifted towards the surf, where something pale and unrecognizable broke the surface, sending a shiver down her spine.

## Scene 2

She waded into the shallows, her eyes scanning the horizon for any sign of movement, but the sky was a deep, foreboding grey, devoid of even the faintest glimmer of life. The tide had retreated far enough to reveal something pale and unrecognizable, half-buried in the sand. Mara's heart quickened as she approached it, her hand reaching out to grasp the object. As she pulled it free from the mud, the waterlogged pages of a logbook lay flat across its surface.

She brushed away the grains of sand, revealing the brass compass that had been buried with it, its surface tarnished but still recognizable. A small inscription was etched into the metal: "Property of Eben Waters, 1850". Mara's gaze followed the compass to the logbook itself, its leather binding cracked and worn. She opened it, releasing a musty scent into the air, and began to read.

The entries were sparse, but they told a story of a life on the water, of storms and sea sickness, of endless days at sea. As Mara turned the pages, her eyes scanned the dates, each one bringing her closer to the night Tomas had vanished. August 15th, 1852 – a storm raged in, darkness closed in. The words seemed to leap off the page, echoing the sailor's own account. She felt a shiver run down her spine as she read on, sensing that she was not alone in this tale of loss and fate.

## Scene 3

Mara's fingers trailed along the edge of the page, her eyes tracing the familiar script. As she read on, the story unfolded like a twisted thread, weaving in and out of Tomas's own narrative. The dates blurred together – August 15th, 1852, December 10th, 1853 – each one a faint echo of another. She felt the weight of his solitude, his struggles against the unyielding sea.

Her thoughts strayed to the nights she'd lain awake, listening for the foghorn's mournful wail. It was as if the instrument itself had been silenced, leaving only an unsettling stillness. Mara knew it was impossible – the horn was supposed to be switched off during the night, a concession to the quiet reverence of those who worshipped at the old church. Yet on certain nights, when the moon hung low in the sky and the wind whispered secrets through the reeds, she swore she could hear its haunting melody.

No one else seemed to notice it – her family, her friends, they all went about their days as if oblivious to this strange nocturnal music. Mara had kept her secret hidden for years, afraid of being ridiculed or pitied. But in this logbook, with Tomas's own words echoing off the page, she felt a spark of connection. As if, together, they might unravel some hidden truth, buried beneath the waves and the silence.

## Scene 4

The pages turned, one by one, like secrets slowly revealed to her eyes. Mara's fingers danced across the yellowed paper, tracing the faded ink and worn edges of each entry. She felt Tomas's presence with every word, his thoughts spilling out onto the page like a confession. As she delved deeper into the logbook, the entries grew more sporadic, the handwriting more erratic – it was as if Tomas had given up, or lost hope.

But one entry stood out, its words scrawled in a frenzy of ink and desperation: "Coordinates...may be our only lead...can't shake the feeling we're running out of time." Mara's heart quickened as she read on, her eyes following the path of Tomas's trembling hand. The numbers seemed to blur together at first – latitude and longitude, impossible to decipher without a chart or compass to guide her.

As she focused in, the handwriting slowed, steadied. In bold strokes, Tomas had written: "43° 27' N - 66° 32' W." Mara's grip on the logbook tightened. This was it – this was where they'd gone down. The ship, the boat... everything that had been lost to the waves for so long. A spark of excitement kindled within her; she felt a thrill of anticipation, mixed with trepidation.

## Scene 5

The air was heavy with expectation as Mara stood by the window, her back to the room, gazing out at the darkness. It was that time of year again – the anniversary of the disappearance. She couldn't shake the feeling that the silence outside was more oppressive than usual, like a held breath waiting for its release. The compass on the shelf seemed to mock her still, its numbers refusing to budge, taunting her with their secrets.

She turned away from the window and made her way to the small kitchenette, where she kept Tomas's logbook wrapped in a faded scarf. Unfurling it, she ran her fingers over the yellowed pages, feeling the weight of history within its worn covers. As she delved into the entries, a sense of unease began to seep into her bones – every word seemed to whisper warnings, echoes of a disaster that had claimed so much.

Outside, the fog rolled in like a damp shroud, casting the room in an eerie twilight. The silence was absolute, broken only by the distant thrum of a foghorn, its mournful wail echoing through the night air like a lost soul's cry. Mara's skin prickled; it was as if the sound itself had never been meant for human ears. She felt eyes upon her, and though there was no one in the room with her, she couldn't shake the sense that someone – or something – was watching from just beyond the darkness.

## Scene 6

Mara stood by the logbook's worn leather cover, her fingers tracing the creased spine as if searching for a hidden truth. The fire had burned down to embers, casting an orange glow over the dimly lit room. Outside, the fog rolled in like a living entity, shrouding everything in its path. She'd lost count of the days since Tomas's disappearance; time had become fluid, measured only by the silence.

The anniversary was approaching, and with it, a sense of unease settled over her like a shroud. The logbook seemed to whisper secrets to her on quiet nights, but she knew better than to trust its tales. Mara opened the cover once more, running her thumb over the yellowed pages, searching for the familiar scratch of Tomas's handwriting. Instead, she found only a few scribbled notes about the weather.

As she delved deeper into the logbook, her fingers stumbled upon a page that had been torn from its moorings. A scrawled set of coordinates seemed to leap off the page, like a splash of ink in the otherwise blank space. Mara's heart quickened; Tomas must have written these when he was planning his journey. But why had he left it here? The weight of the logbook felt heavier now, as if it held secrets she was not meant to uncover.

A faint scratching sound echoed from outside, like a hesitant knock on the door. Mara froze, her heart pounding in anticipation. Who – or what – could be visiting at this late hour?

## Scene 7

Mara's eyes were fixed on the door as the scratching grew louder, taking on a rhythmic quality that seemed almost deliberate. Her heart pounded in her chest, the tension building as she wondered who could be visiting at this late hour. The foghorn sound echoed once more in the distance, its mournful wail lingering in the air like a ghostly presence. It was a night when the sound should not have been audible, yet there it was, a constant reminder of the mysteries that lurked within these walls.

As she hesitated, her gaze drifted to the logbook still clutched in her hand. The weight of its secrets seemed to grow heavier by the minute, and Mara felt an overwhelming urge to hide it once more, to lock away the truth that Tomas had so unwittingly revealed. But something about this knock – hesitant, almost nervous – made her pause.

With a sudden jolt, she rose from her chair and approached the door. Her hand trembled as she grasped the handle, her mind racing with possibilities. Slowly, she turned it, and the creaking of the door seemed to echo through the silence like a betrayal. A sliver of moonlight illuminated the entrance hall, casting an eerie glow on the figure standing in the shadows. It was old Mr. Jenkins, the lighthouse keeper's predecessor, his eyes sunken with age as he regarded Mara with a knowing gaze. "I see you're reading from Tomas's logbook again," he said, his voice barely above a whisper. "The foghorn sounds like it's been calling to me tonight."

## Scene 8

Mara hesitated, her eyes darting to Mr. Jenkins as if seeking permission to continue. The old man's gaze never wavered, his eyes seeming to bore into her very soul. She took a deep breath and nodded, flipping the page of the logbook over to reveal the torn entry.

As she smoothed out the creased paper, the faint scent of saltwater wafted up, carrying with it memories of Tomas's stories. Mara's heart quickened, her mind racing with possibilities. The coordinates, scribbled in a shaky hand, seemed to leap off the page, demanding attention.

"Tomas wrote these down," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "He was convinced that his boat had gone down here."

Mr. Jenkins nodded, his eyes lighting up with a deep understanding. "The foghorn's been sounding for weeks, calling out like a mournful spirit. I've been trying to decipher the message, but it's always eluded me."

Mara's gaze locked onto the coordinates once more, her mind racing with the implications. She felt an inexplicable sense of connection to this place, as if Tomas had left behind a piece of himself here.

Without another word, Mara stood, the logbook still clutched in her hand. Mr. Jenkins watched her, his eyes following her movement as she strode towards the door. With a quiet resolve, she grasped the brass compass that hung on the hook by the entrance hall and began to walk towards the foghorn, its mournful call echoing out into the darkness like a siren's warning.
